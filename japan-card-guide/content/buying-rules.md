# Buying Pokémon cards in Japan: the 2026 rules for visitors

## New sets usually sell out on day one

Popular Japanese sets often disappear from shelves within hours of release, and restocks are unpredictable. Plan your trip around **what you can realistically find** (older sets, singles at card shops, Pokémon Center exclusives) rather than counting on a brand-new box at retail price.

## Purchase limits are normal

Many stores cap how many packs or boxes one customer can buy, and convenience store chains often set per-checkout limits for popular releases. Limits are usually posted at the register, and staff will not make exceptions.

## Some shops sell new sets only to children

Some card shops reserve part of their stock for children — sometimes checking ID, and sometimes refusing purchases made on a child's behalf, even by parents. If you see a sign mentioning 中学生以下 ("junior high and younger"), 小学生 ("elementary school students") or 学生限定 ("students only"), the product is not for adult buyers.

## Official lotteries are hard for tourists to enter

The most sought-after products are sold through online lotteries (抽選, *chūsen*). Since August 2026, priority entry for some Pokémon Center Online lotteries requires an account verified with Japan's My Number Card through the government's Digital Authentication App — and for the 30th anniversary products, most winning slots went to verified accounts. Because the My Number Card is issued only to residents of Japan, visitors are effectively shut out of these lotteries.

## Pack prices went up in 2026

Standard Japanese Pokémon booster packs from sets released from May 2026 onward cost **¥200** (previously ¥180), so a 30-pack box is **¥6,000** at retail. Special sets can cost more — packs of the 30th anniversary expansion are ¥360. Anything priced far above retail is a resale price.

## Tax-free shopping

Japan's tax-free system changes on **November 1, 2026**. At participating stores you now pay the price **including** consumption tax and show your passport; the tax is refunded when customs confirms at your departure airport or seaport that you're taking the goods out of Japan. You must complete the check at a customs kiosk within **90 days** of purchase. Registering on Visit Japan Web before your trip makes the process smoother. Items you use up in Japan aren't eligible, refund fees vary by store, and card shops that sell singles may not offer tax-free sales at all — ask before you pay.

## Etiquette that keeps shops friendly

- Queue where staff tell you to, and don't wait outside before opening if signs ask you not to.
- Don't open packs at the counter or search ("weigh") packs on the shelf.
- Respect limits even if you're traveling as a group.
- Buying to resell abroad is exactly what these rules are designed to stop — shops may refuse sales if they suspect it.
