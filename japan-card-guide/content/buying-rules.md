# Buying Pokémon cards in Japan: the 2026 rules for visitors

## New sets usually sell out on day one

Popular Japanese sets often disappear from shelves within hours of release, and restocks are unpredictable. Plan your trip around **what you can realistically find** (older sets, singles at card shops, Pokémon Center exclusives) rather than counting on a brand-new box at retail price.

## Purchase limits are normal

Many stores cap how many packs or boxes one customer can buy. Some convenience store chains have set per-checkout limits for popular releases — for example, five packs per checkout for an anniversary set. [[VERIFY: ローソンの記念セット「1会計5パックまで」の事例が現在も正しいか]] Limits are posted at the register; staff will not make exceptions.

## Some shops sell new sets only to children

At least one well-known Akihabara card shop has limited its newest sets to junior-high-school students and younger, with a daily pack limit. [[VERIFY: 晴れる屋2の「中学生以下限定」の販売方針が現在も続いているか]] If you see a sign mentioning 中学生以下 ("junior high and younger") or 小学生 ("elementary school students"), the product is not for adult buyers.

## Official lotteries are hard for tourists to enter

The most sought-after products are sold through online lotteries (抽選, *chūsen*). In 2026 The Pokémon Company announced identity checks using Japan's My Number Card for some Pokémon Center Online lotteries. [[VERIFY: マイナンバーカードでの本人確認が2026年8月に予定どおり始まったか、対象商品の範囲]] Because the My Number Card is issued only to residents of Japan, most visitors effectively cannot enter these lotteries.

## Pack prices went up in 2026

Standard Japanese booster packs from sets released from May 2026 onward cost **¥200** (previously ¥180), so a 30-pack box is about **¥6,000** at retail. Anything priced far above that is a resale price.

## Tax-free shopping

Visitors can usually shop tax-free at participating stores when spending above a minimum amount, by showing their passport. [[VERIFY: 2026年11月からの免税制度の変更（リファンド方式）の内容と開始日]] Card shops that sell singles may not offer tax-free sales — ask before you pay.

## Etiquette that keeps shops friendly

- Queue where staff tell you to, and don't wait outside before opening if signs ask you not to.
- Don't open packs at the counter or search ("weigh") packs on the shelf.
- Respect limits even if you're traveling as a group.
- Buying to resell abroad is exactly what these rules are designed to stop — shops may refuse sales if they suspect it.
